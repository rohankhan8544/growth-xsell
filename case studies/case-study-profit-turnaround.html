About the brand

A profitable revenue story hiding behind a bad margin mix.

The brand was already generating meaningful revenue, but the account was leaking profit in the wrong places. Revenue looked healthy on paper, while the real issue was product economics and campaign efficiency.

That meant the business was not failing because it was small — it was failing because the money coming in was being spent too expensively and too broadly. Many of the account's top sellers were carrying too much ad cost, and pricing wasn't supporting a healthy margin structure.

What we took over: PPC structure, spend allocation, product-level economics, bid strategy, and pricing guardrails.

The challenge

Revenue was moving, but profit was being burned.

The brand had enough demand to grow, but the account structure was making it hard to tell which SKUs and campaigns were creating real value versus just burning ad spend.

01

Weak product economics

Some products were profitable on paper and still not sustainable once ads, fees, and shipping were considered.

02

Ad spend was too blunt

Budget was being distributed without enough attention to profitability by product, keyword, and search intent.

03

Low margin for error

At a negative net margin, every inefficient click or underpriced unit reduced financial performance quickly.

04

Growth was not truly healthy

The business could buy revenue, but it could not buy profit. The problem was operational, not demand-driven.

What they needed

More than a better campaign setup — a healthier profit model.

Objective: fix the margin leak without reducing top-line revenue, and turn ad efficiency into profit growth.
  • Identify which products and keywords were actually creating profit
  • Reduce waste in broad, low-intent spend
  • Improve bid strategy around contribution margin, not just clicks
  • Use pricing and product positioning to protect margin
  • Keep growth compounding without a revenue tradeoff
What we found

The issue was not demand. It was the economics behind it.

Before changing anything, we read the account as a profit system — not a marketing dashboard. That meant looking at revenue, margin, PPC efficiency, and how SKU-level performance translated into real profit.

Some campaigns were converting but not contributing.

A few campaigns were producing sales, but at a cost structure that erased the benefit. We needed to separate high-volume activity from high-value profit activity.

Product-level spend was misallocated.

Budget was concentrated on products that looked strong in top-line sales but were actually dragging margin down once ad costs and fee pressure were included.

Efficiency was lagging behind the real opportunity.

The account had room to grow without adding waste; the quick win was to improve how spend was deployed, not to cut the business back.

The strategy

We rebuilt the account around profit, not just volume.

01

Repriced the margin mix

We corrected pricing and product-level economics so more sales could land profitably instead of just selling at a loss.

02

Changed PPC behavior

Campaigns were tightened to prioritize strong contribution margin, with smarter bids and less spend on weak-intent traffic.

03

Protected volume growth

We kept the account moving upward without letting the growth engine consume profit. The goal was stronger revenue quality, not reduced scale.

The outcome

A turn from margin loss to consistent monthly profit.
