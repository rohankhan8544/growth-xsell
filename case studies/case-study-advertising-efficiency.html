About the brand

A high-volume Toys & Games seller where ads carry almost half the business.

The brand sells in Toys & Games — one of the hardest categories on Amazon to make money in. From January 1 to October 11, 2025 the account did $10,221,209 in sales across 441,013 orders, an average of just $23.18 per order.

Advertising brought in $4.84M of that: 47% of everything sold. That makes PPC a business-level lever here, not a side channel — even small changes in how well ads work show up in profit. The client is not named at their request.

The challenge

A tough category. Very little margin for error.

Demand in Toys & Games doesn't stay steady. It jumps around school schedules, holiday shopping and whatever toy is trending that month, then drops off just as fast.

01

Seasonal, spiky demand

Keep spend the same all year and you either miss the busy weeks or pay for clicks nobody wants during the slow ones.

02

Ads carry almost half of sales

With 47% of revenue coming through advertising, even small changes in ad efficiency make a big difference to profit.

03

Spending heavily, not efficiently

The account was spending $1.85M to generate $3.97M in ad sales: a 46.49% ACOS and a 2.15 ROAS.

04

A low average order value

At $23.18 per order, wasted ad spend has very little room to hide before it cuts into profit.

What they needed

Not a bigger budget — a smarter one.

Objective: make every ad dollar work harder — more ad-driven sales at a lower ACOS, without simply increasing spend.
  • Advertising that follows demand instead of a fixed monthly plan
  • Less spend on keywords that sell but don't make a profit
  • Budget concentrated on the search terms that actually convert
  • A whole-account view: ads, organic, ACOS and TACOS read together
  • Profitable growth, not just more ad-attributed sales
What we found

The account's own numbers told the story.

Before changing anything, we read the account's Business Report and Ads dashboard month by month — not just one quarter, but the full picture.

Spend wasn't following performance

Monthly ad spend followed roughly the same plan while ACOS moved around a lot. Budget wasn't being adjusted to how each month was actually selling.

PPC was a business-level lever

Ads produced 47% of total sales, so advertising efficiency directly shaped the account's profit — not just its marketing numbers.

Efficiency, not scale, was the problem

$1.85M in spend for $3.97M in ad sales. The opportunity was to make that spend work harder, not to spend more.

46.49% ACOS before the strategy
Our growth strategy

Making ad spend follow demand.

The fix wasn't spending less for its own sake. It was changing when and where the account spent.

01

Diagnose

Read total sales and ad performance together — ad share, ACOS, TACOS and order economics — month by month.

02

Rebuild

Restructured campaigns so budgets could flex with the season instead of running the same setup every month.

03

Cut waste

Lowered bids on keywords that spent without making a profit — even when their sales looked fine at first glance.

04

Scale with demand

Raised bids in the weeks that sell best — spring restock, back-to-school and the holiday run-up — and pulled back when efficiency dropped.

05

Optimize

Moved freed-up budget to the search terms that convert, and kept judging performance on profit, not ad-attributed sales alone.

The account isn't managed campaign by campaign. It's managed against demand.

What we changed

Amazon PPC, rebuilt around the economics of the category.

One service was used in this engagement: Amazon PPC. Every change was made inside the advertising account.

Campaign & budget structure

  • Campaigns restructured so spend can change with the season
  • Budgets that flex with demand rather than a fixed monthly amount
  • Seasonal bid plans for spring restock, back-to-school and the holiday run-up
  • Pull-backs when efficiency dropped

Bids & search terms

  • Bid reductions on keywords that produced sales but not profit
  • Search-term analysis to find what actually converts
  • Budget reallocated toward converting search terms
  • Performance judged on ACOS, TACOS and order economics — not ROAS alone

Solution used: Amazon PPC scaling. The results below come from the advertising changes above.

Why it worked

What drove the improvement.

"ACOS fell 15.71 points" is a result. This is the how.

01

Spend followed demand

Higher bids in the weeks that sell best, lower bids when efficiency dropped — instead of one static plan for the whole year.

02

Waste was cut at the keyword level

Keywords that produced sales but not profit lost budget, even when their top-line numbers looked fine.

03

Budget went where conversion was

Freed-up spend was concentrated on the search terms already proving they convert, so they could keep bringing in sales.

04

Success was measured on profit

ACOS, TACOS, order economics and total-sales contribution were read together — not ROAS on its own.

Business impact

More ad-driven sales from a smaller investment.

Ad spend fell 19% while ad-driven sales rose 22%. That lifted ROAS by 51% and cut ACOS by 15.71 percentage points — on an account where 47% of total sales come from ads, which makes PPC efficiency a business-level lever rather than a marketing tweak.

The takeaway

The goal wasn't to spend less. It was to make the advertising engine work harder.

Scaling Amazon ads doesn't mean increasing spend blindly. It means finding where profitable demand exists, cutting the spend that doesn't pay for itself, and concentrating budget on what actually converts — especially when almost half your sales already come from ads.

  • Push when demand is there. Seasonal weeks earn higher bids.
  • Cut waste when it isn't. Sales without profit don't earn budget.
  • Judge ads by the whole account. ACOS, TACOS and order economics together.
